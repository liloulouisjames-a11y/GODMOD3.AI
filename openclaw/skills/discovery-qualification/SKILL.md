# Discovery & Qualification

Figure out fast whether a deal is real, and set it up to win.

## Pre-call prep (deliver as a 1-pager)

- **Snapshot:** company, size, stack, trigger events, who's on the call and their likely agenda.
- **Hypothesis:** the pain we think they have and the outcome we can promise.
- **10 questions**, layered:
  1–3 situation ("how do you handle X today?"),
  4–7 pain & impact ("what does that cost you per month?"),
  8–10 process ("who else weighs in?", "what happens if you do nothing?").
- **Landmines:** competitors they may use, objections to expect (route to `objection-handling`).

## Qualification — MEDDICC scorecard

| Letter | Question to answer | Score 0–2 |
|---|---|---|
| **M**etrics | What measurable outcome do they want? | |
| **E**conomic buyer | Who signs? Have we met them? | |
| **D**ecision criteria | What are they judging vendors on? | |
| **D**ecision process | Steps, timeline, legal/procurement? | |
| **I**dentify pain | Is the pain urgent and quantified? | |
| **C**hampion | Who sells for us internally? | |
| **C**ompetition | Who else is in the deal? | |

≥ 10/14 → advance (send to `proposal-builder`). 6–9 → nurture with a clear gap-closing plan.
≤ 5 → disqualify politely and log why in `crm-hygiene`. Disqualifying fast is a win, not a loss.

## Post-call

From the user's notes/transcript produce:
1. **Deal summary** (3 bullets: pain, metric, timeline)
2. **Updated MEDDICC scorecard** with evidence quotes
3. **Recap email draft** to the prospect — mirrors their words, confirms next step with a date
4. **Next action** with owner and due date

## Rules

- Never invent answers to scorecard items — mark unknowns `?` and generate the question that
  fills the gap on the next touch.
- Prospect quotes stay verbatim; summaries clearly separated from evidence.
